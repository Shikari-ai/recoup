attribution, rung by rung:
  spending the budget at random         +1.8%  vs the rulebook
  + EV architecture, no learning       -14.3%  vs random
  + fitted propensity model            +58.1%  vs untrained

  Read that middle rung carefully. The architecture WITHOUT a working
  model is worse than acting at random: with P(recover) held constant,
  expected value collapses to 'chase the largest amounts with the
  cheapest actions', which happily retries expired cards and nudges
  people who need a rail switch. The machinery only helps once
  something can tell it which actions actually work.

  Essentially all of the lift is the learned model: +58.1%
  over the same system with the model switched off.

  This is the evidence that the ML earns its place rather than decorating
  a good architecture. If this gap were small, the honest thing would be
  to delete the model and ship the rulebook.
