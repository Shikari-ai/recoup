The ceiling is low because the problem is genuinely noisy, not because
the model is weak. Recovery is a coin flip weighted by context: two
receivables with a 30% and a 40% chance will disagree with their
probabilities often enough to cap AUC near 0.78 for ANY model.

Note that OBSERVABLE-ONLY sits essentially on top of ORACLE. The latent
per-customer traits contribute almost nothing to rankability, so the
model is not starved of information -- it is bounded by noise. That is
why adding features to close the gap did not work.

Training-set scaling: estimation error, or model-class bias?
====================================================================

 train events  train rows   model AUC    oracle   captured
----------------------------------------------------------
        3,000       6,668      0.7577    0.7866     89.9%
        8,000      23,905      0.7628    0.7866     91.7%
       20,000      65,582      0.7670    0.7866     93.2%
       45,000     150,232      0.7689    0.7866     93.8%
----------------------------------------------------------

Captured signal rises 89.9% -> 93.8% as rows accumulate,
then plateaus. So roughly 4% of the gap is estimation error that
more merchant history closes, and roughly 6% is bias in the
model class -- a linear-in-log-odds form cannot represent every
interaction the world contains.

That residual is the price of the modelling choice, and it is small:
a richer model class could recover about 6% of the achievable
ranking signal, worth a few thousandths of AUC. It would cost the exact
signed decomposition of every decision that makes this agent auditable.
Stated as a number, that trade is easy to defend; stated as a preference,
it is not.
